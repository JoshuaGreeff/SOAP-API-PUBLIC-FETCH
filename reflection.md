# Reflection

I began by choosing the InterSystems SOAP Demonstration Service from the provided WSDL list and testing its `AddInteger` operation with Zeep. I then placed that call behind Flask GET and POST routes, added range validation for the two submitted numbers, and checked that the returned SOAP value was also an integer in the expected range.

Zeep was the part I struggled with most because it feels very different from the REST APIs used in most coding classes. With REST, I am used to choosing a URL and HTTP method, sending JSON, and reading a JSON response. With Zeep, the WSDL contract tells the client which operations exist and even the parameter names, such as `Arg1` and `Arg2`. At first, that felt less direct, but inspecting the WSDL made it clearer why the client knew how to build the SOAP request. Troubleshooting also helped me understand the difference between my local validation errors and an error returned by the SOAP service. I now better understand how WSDL-driven clients convert XML responses into Python values, although I would like more practice with complex SOAP objects and faults.
